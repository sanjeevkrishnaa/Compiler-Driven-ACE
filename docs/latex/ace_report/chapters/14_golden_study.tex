\chapter{Fresh Golden Study: Contract and Execution Record}

This chapter is the living record for the consolidated experiment program. It is
kept separate from historical ACE results so that a result cannot be promoted merely
because it exists in an older output directory. Every accepted observation must have
an immutable trace hash, a configuration manifest, paired seeds, complete requests,
and a source CSV listed in the study registry.

The cross-policy headline metric is request eligibility-to-EPR-delivery latency. It
includes admission wait, reservation, retry, and generation time. It excludes time
before eligibility during which a cached pair was merely stored. Whole-program cost
is reported separately as makespan.

The planned capacity study includes constrained settings and deliberately labelled
architecture-scaling settings. Capacities greater than the current four-memory model
are sensitivity points, not claims about the baseline hardware. The torus arm uses
Dijkstra routing only. Mesh routing comparisons hold a placement contract fixed.

The ten workload inputs are frozen QASM files recovered with SHA-256 provenance
from the historical MQT study archive. They are input evidence, not recycled
observations: fresh T-HQA placements (and the HQA control placements) are generated
from this corpus into immutable placement contracts before route expansion. The
mapping tool has no implicit workload, seed, topology, or routing default. It refuses
execution when the declared QASM parser is absent rather than selecting another
parser. At the time the contract scaffold was created, that parser dependency was
absent, so no mapping result was recorded until the fresh mapper stage could run.

The parser was subsequently installed in the project environment and the fresh
mapping stage completed. The mapping audit validated all 300 derived route
artifacts (placement hash, input hash, endpoints, adjacency, migration count and
hop count). Table~\ref{tab:golden-mapping-control} is the mesh--XY mapper-only
control; its exact machine-readable source is named in the caption. It does not
imply an ACE latency result.

Across the ten frozen inputs, T-HQA produces 16,758 mesh--XY hops compared with
HQA's 19,079, while producing 7,702 versus 7,587 logical migrations. This is a
trade-off to evaluate with ACE, not a declaration of universal superiority.
Crucially, the three mapper/reliability seeds yielded one unique placement for each
workload/algorithm/topology; they must be treated as reliability-route checks, not
independent placement replications.

The base evaluation matrix is now frozen. Deterministic XY and YX routes are
byte-identical across the three mapper/reliability seeds, whereas shortest-reliable,
maximum-reliability, and weighted torus Dijkstra routes change with their seeded
reliability maps. Exact trace-hash deduplication therefore leaves 110 distinct traces
from 150 conceptual aliases. Table~\ref{tab:golden-execution-plan} records the full
base workload after enumerating all registered memory allocations and 30 paired
physics seeds.

The congestion-adaptive threshold remains a separate training-only decision. It
will enumerate every behaviorally distinct observed layer-activity threshold using
seeds 30--39, freeze one rule, and only then evaluate seeds 0--29. This chapter
contains no performance conclusion until the corresponding raw CSV files and
validation records exist.

The first accepted matrix slice is the zero-cache boundary for GHZ on mesh--XY
with two communication memories per core. Table~\ref{tab:golden-progress-zero} is
generated directly from the validated raw job directories. Its five policies are
expected to match because no policy may retain a pre-generated pair when $C=0$;
this is a boundary invariant, not evidence that the policies are equivalent at
positive cache capacity.

Table~\ref{tab:golden-progress-positive} is the first positive-cache comparison.
On this sparse GHZ trace, full-chain Dynamic has the lowest mean latency and
makespan; its mean latency is 50.10\% below ODG. First-hop Dynamic is 42.58\%
below ODG, and CGP/ACGP are 21.66\% below ODG. This one cell is evidence that the
implementations can benefit from pre-generation, but it is not a cross-workload
or routing conclusion.

The remainder of the GHZ mesh--XY capacity sweep is now complete for every total
memory value and every legal compiler allocation. The analysis first validates the
entire triangular $(M,C)$ grid and all 30 seeds, then reports the lowest observed
mean-latency allocation for each policy and $M$. This is a descriptive envelope of
the registered grid, not a claim that evaluation data have trained a universally
optimal allocation. Figure~\ref{fig:golden-ghz-xy-all-allocations} retains all
allocation points so that the envelope cannot hide an adverse region.

Dynamic full-chain reaches its latency floor with two compiler memories: from
$M=3$ onward its mean request latency is 0.46320~ms, 66.11\% below ODG, with
100\% intended-request hits, no on-demand fallback, and no observed compiler or
protocol waste. Dynamic first-hop saturates with one compiler memory at
0.78488~ms because its intended hit rate is 62.5\% and its fallback rate is
37.5\%. At $M\geq16$, CGP and ACGP reach 0.46250~ms, only 0.00069~ms (about
0.15\%) below Dynamic full-chain, but their delivered fidelity is approximately
0.852--0.854 rather than 0.94159. They also require at least 13--17 compiler
memories in these observed plateaus. Thus Dynamic full-chain is the strongest
latency--capacity--fidelity trade-off in this sparse slice, while the high-capacity
CGP/ACGP point has the absolute minimum latency. No cross-workload claim follows
until the remaining registered slices are complete.

The second complete slice repeats the same exhaustive memory grid for BV without
changing the simulator or physical parameters.

\input{generated/golden_bv_mesh_xy_memory_sweep}

For BV, Dynamic full-chain reaches the exact 0.44400~ms latency floor and
736.10~ms makespan with $C=2$ from $M=3$ onward. This is 67.05\% below the
1.34737~ms ODG request latency. Its intended-request hit rate is 100\%, its
on-demand fallback rate is zero, and no compiler or protocol waste is observed.
CGP and ACGP first tie that latency at $M=32$, using $C=18$; at $M=64$ their
smallest tied allocations are respectively 17 and 15. Their corresponding
delivered fidelity is approximately 0.781--0.782, roughly 0.160 below Dynamic
full-chain's 0.94168. At the latency-minimum points in
Table~\ref{tab:golden-bv-xy-envelope}, CGP/ACGP protocol waste spans
58.36--71.79\%, while Dynamic full-chain has zero observed protocol and compiler
waste. Unlike GHZ, CGP and ACGP differ at intermediate capacities, demonstrating
why conclusions must be checked workload by workload.

\section{QFT-96 focused mesh and torus study}

QFT-96 is a separate 96-qubit mapper-generated circuit; the ten-workload Golden
corpus contains a different 48-qubit QFT input. The QFT-96 study uses T-HQA
placement with mapper seeds 42--44. Each placement is held fixed when exporting
mesh XY, YX, shortest-reliable and maximum-reliability routes and torus
Dijkstra routes. This makes the topology comparison a change in routing and
physical connectivity on the same logical migrations. The QFT-96 route audit
checks all 15 exports, including endpoint correctness, adjacency, trace hashes
and replay to the original placement. The two-seed ACE execution plan includes
all eight declared memory capacities, every legal compiler allocation, ODG,
CGP, ACGP, Dynamic full-chain and Dynamic first-hop. Its simulator results are
reported only as each matched cell validates; no scheduler ranking follows from
the structural figure below.

\section{Registered MQT Bench QPE extension}

The frozen ten-workload base matrix is not mutated when a new benchmark is added.
Instead, the separately versioned QPE extension uses MQT Bench's \texttt{qpeexact}
factory at 48 logical qubits. Its generated OpenQASM has an immutable SHA-256
digest, and records a circuit depth of 143 and 1,293 operations. Fresh HQA and
T-HQA placements were generated for all three mapper seeds and both topologies;
the mapping audit validated all 30 route artifacts for endpoint correctness,
adjacency, input hash and partition provenance. Mesh evaluates XY, YX,
shortest-reliable and maximum-reliability; torus uses Dijkstra only. The execution
plan below has 15 conceptual aliases, 11 exact-hash-distinct traces, 3,058 runner
jobs and 180,840 policy-seed simulations. It is a controlled extension, not a
retroactive change to the base-study denominator. The distinct MQT Bench
\texttt{qpeinexact} factory is deliberately not silently conflated with this exact
QPE benchmark.
