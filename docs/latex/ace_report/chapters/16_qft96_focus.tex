\chapter{QFT-96 focused results and run ledger}
\label{ch:qft96-focus}

This chapter is the single location for the current QFT-96 investigation. It
is intentionally separate from the historical multi-workload chapters. QFT-96
is a 96-logical-qubit, 766-layer circuit generated by the mapper workflow and
placed with T-HQA at mapping seeds 42, 43 and 44. The original logical
migrations are held fixed when comparing routing and topology: mesh uses XY,
YX, shortest-reliable and maximum-reliability; torus uses Dijkstra only.

The route exporter validated all 15 conceptual route artifacts for migration
endpoints, adjacent-hop validity, trace hashes and replay to the placement.
There are 11 byte-distinct traces after exact deduplication. The base ACE phase
enumerates memories $M\in\{2,3,4,6,8,16,32\}$, every legal compiler
allocation $C=0,\ldots,M-1$, ODG, CGP, ACGP, Dynamic full-chain and Dynamic
first-hop, with physics seeds 0 and 1. A separate Dynamic-policy phase fixes
$M=4$, $C=3$ and evaluates the 13 launch-policy variants listed in the research
ledger. These variants are grouped into structural policies and auxiliary delay
sensitivities; the latter are not treated as separate algorithms. The formal
definitions and later multi-machine execution contract are in
\href{https://github.com/sanjeevkrishnaa/Compiler-Driven-ACE/blob/codex/ace-scheduler-correctness/docs/research/qft96-experimental-design.md}{the QFT-96 experimental-design contract}.
All timing uses the corrected arrival-to-completion simulator metric;
the historical 5.3-ms subtraction is not used.

The initially generated plan included $M=64$. On 26 October 2026, after 425
of its 1,419 jobs had completed, the scope was explicitly revised to stop at
$M=32$. The original manifest and raw $M=64$ outputs remain available for
provenance, but the partial $M=64$ subset is excluded from aggregate tables,
figures and conclusions. The official filtered base manifest contains 1,639
jobs. This avoids presenting an execution-order-biased partial endpoint as a
complete result.

\section{QFT-96 congestion and route heatmaps}

Congestion is analysed independently from physical timing so that the route
effect is not confounded with simulator scheduling. The analysis covers all
15 T-HQA mapping/routing cells, capacities $2,3,4,6,8,16,32$, and both
counting definitions. In hop counting, an internal relay contributes two
incident demands (arrival and departure); in route counting, a qubit route
contributes one demand at each core it visits. For every cell and capacity we
record total overload, source/destination overload, relay-only overload, excess
demand, and per-core relay share. Torus traces are parsed with wraparound-aware
structural validation, rather than the mesh-only adjacency validator.

The generated heatmaps contain three panels per trace: total overload,
source/destination overload, and relay-only overload. Percentages are over the
226 communication-active layers of the 766-layer QFT trace; idle layers are not
silently counted as zero congestion. The complete CSVs and 210 route-specific
heatmaps are linked below. The congestion pass is trace-level and therefore is
valid before all ACE timing jobs finish.

\section{Interpretation boundary}

No claim that Dynamic is generally better is made from the checkpoint or from
the partial ledger. Final conclusions require all base cells and all 13
launch-policy variants to validate. The separate congestion-adaptive Dynamic
policy is not silently folded into this phase: it requires a QFT-96-specific
training split and a frozen threshold before evaluation. When that training
phase is complete, its manifest and results will be added here as another
explicit row rather than changing the meaning of existing rows.
