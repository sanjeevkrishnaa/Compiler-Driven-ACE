\chapter{QFT-96 completed study}
\label{ch:qft96-focus}

This chapter is the final, self-contained record of the QFT-96 study. QFT-96
has 96 logical qubits and 766 circuit layers. Its mappings use T-HQA with
mapping seeds 42, 43 and 44. The logical migration endpoints are held fixed
while routes are changed: mesh uses XY, YX, shortest-reliable and
maximum-reliability; torus uses Dijkstra. All timing below uses the corrected
arrival-to-completion request metric. It includes admission and memory waiting
after a request is eligible, but does not charge a request for cached-pair
storage before eligibility. The historical 5.3-ms lead subtraction is absent.

The route exporter validated all 15 conceptual route artifacts for endpoint
replay, adjacent-hop validity and trace hashes. Exact deduplication leaves 11
byte-distinct traces; this is why XY/YX have fewer independent trace
observations than reliability-sensitive routes. The base phase covers
$M\in\{2,3,4,6,8,16,32\}$, every legal compiler allocation
$C=0,\ldots,M-1$, ODG, CGP, ACGP, Dynamic full-chain and Dynamic first-hop,
with physics seeds 0 and 1. The Dynamic-policy phase fixes $M=4,C=3$ and
evaluates thirteen explicitly named launch variants. The original $M=64$ plan
was stopped before completion and is excluded from every table, figure and
claim in this chapter.

\section{Route structure and congestion}
\input{generated/qft96_route_hops}

Congestion is a structural demand analysis, deliberately separate from ACE
time evolution. For each communication-active layer, \emph{route occupancy}
counts a qubit once at every core on its route; \emph{hop incidence} counts an
internal relay twice, once on arrival and once on departure. ``Relay-only''
means total demand exceeds capacity while endpoint demand does not. The
denominator is the 226 communication-active layers, not all 766 circuit
layers. Thus these maps show where a route creates pressure; they do not claim
that a core was physically occupied for that entire fraction of time.

\section{Dynamic launch-policy study}

The 13-policy sweep separates algorithmic launch choices from delay
sensitivities. The three structural choices are: layer-start (prepare at the
planned generation layer), predecessor-start (start a later hop when its
predecessor begins), and first-hop-only (pre-generate only the first hop of a
qubit chain). The delay variants are retained in the source data as sensitivity
evidence, rather than being misrepresented as independent scheduling
algorithms.

\section{QFT-96 conclusion and boundary}

The completed QFT-96 matrix supports three bounded conclusions. First, routing
changes structural pressure: at $M=4$, torus Dijkstra has lower route-occupancy
overload than the listed mesh examples, while maximum-reliability routing can
substantially increase pressure for some mapping seeds. Second, a cache policy
must be evaluated with admission waiting and fidelity: a pre-generation hit is
not automatically a latency win. Third, in this corrected fixed-memory QFT-96
comparison, Dynamic first-hop is the strongest tested Dynamic structural policy
against ODG on mean request latency; this motivates, but does not replace, the
forthcoming multi-workload study.

No conclusion here covers deferred relay data-qubit storage occupancy, an
untrained congestion-threshold policy, or benchmarks other than QFT-96. Those
remain explicit next experiments rather than silent assumptions.
